{EX|praeclarum|(UITP 2003)}

Let {v|x, y, z, t : Prop}. If {h|x → z} and {h|y → t} then {c|(x ∧ y) → (z ∧ t)}.

  • Suppose {h|x ∧ y}.
    • We get {c|z} by {f|x → z}, since
      • we have {c|x}.
    • We get {c|t} by {f|y → t}, since
      • we have {c|y}.
    Altogether we have {c|z ∧ t}.
{EX|square_pos|(UITP 2003)}

Let {v|a, b ∈ ℤ}. If {h|0 < a < b} then {c|a a < b b}.

  • It is equivalent to prove {c|0 < b b − a a}.
    • That can also be written as {c|0 < (b + a)(b − a)}.
      • From {f|0 < a < b} we have {c|0 < b + a ∧ 0 < b − a}.
{EX|exp_pos|(UITP 2003)}

Let {v|x ∈ ℝ} and {v|y ∈ ℤ}. If {h|0 < x}, to prove {c|0 < xy}, we have three cases:

  • If {h|0 < y} then {c|x1 ≤ xy}.
  • If {h|0 = y} then {c|xy = 1}.
  • If {h|y < 0} then {c|xy = 1 / x−y}.
{EX|praeclarum|(xtext)}

Let {v|x, y, z, t : Prop}. Assume {h|(x → z) ∧ (y → t)} {n|h1} and {h|x ∧ y} {n|h2}. We prove {c|z ∧ t}.

  • Going back to the definition of and ({b|and_ind}) in {b|h1}, we prove {c|z ∧ t}:
    • Assume {h|x → z} {n|i} and {h|y → t} {n|j}. Going back to the definition of and ({b|and_ind}) in {b|h2}, we prove {c|z ∧ t}:
      • Assume {h|x} {n|k} and {h|y} {n|l}.
        • Applying {b|i} to {b|k}, we have {c|z}.
        • Applying {b|j} to {b|l}, we have {c|t}.
        So by definition of and ({b|conj}), we have {c|z ∧ t}.
{EX|Trans_Inv|(xtext)}

Let {v|E : Set}. Let {v|R : Rel E}. Assume {h|Trans E R} {n|h}. We prove {c|Trans E (Inv E R)}.

  • Let us choose arbitrarily {v|x, y, z ∈ E}; assume {h|Inv E R x y} {n|h1} and {h|Inv E R y z} {n|h2}.
    • By {b|h} we have {m|Trans E R}, that is {c|∀ x₀ y₀ z₀ ∈ E, R x₀ y₀ → R y₀ z₀ → R x₀ z₀}.
    • By {b|h2} we have {m|Inv E R y z}, that is {c|R z y}.
    • By {b|h1} we have {m|Inv E R x y}, that is {c|R y x}.
    By application, we have {m|R z x}, that is {c|Inv E R x z}.
  • We have proved {c|∀ x₀ y₀ z₀ ∈ E, Inv E R x₀ y₀ → Inv E R y₀ z₀ → Inv E R x₀ z₀}, that is {m|Trans E (Inv E R)}.
